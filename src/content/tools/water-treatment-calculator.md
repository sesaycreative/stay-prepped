  - q: How long should I boil water to make it safe?
    a: The Drinking Water Inspectorate says to boil water until it reaches a rolling boil, then let it cool naturally. The US CDC says to keep it at a rolling boil for 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 feet (about 1,981 metres).
  - q: How much bleach do I add to a litre of water?
    a: For plain, unscented bleach of 5% to 9% strength, the US CDC gives 2 drops (0.1 ml) per litre of clear water. For 1% bleach, it is 10 drops (0.5 ml) per litre. Double the dose for cloudy water and wait at least 30 minutes.
  - q: What if I don't know the strength of my bleach?
    a: Do not guess. There is no published dose for an unknown strength. Boil the water, use purification tablets as the packet says, or use bottled water.
  - q: Do water filters make water safe?
    a: Not on their own. The US CDC says portable filters will not remove viruses. After filtering, disinfect the water too, by boiling it or using bleach or tablets.

## When to use it

If there is a problem with your tap water, your water company will send a notice to homes in the area. The Drinking Water Inspectorate describes two situations:

- **A boil water notice.** Boil tap water before you drink it, brush your teeth with it, make ice or prepare food with it. You can still shower and bathe, but keep the water out of your mouth.
- **An instruction to stop using your water.** This is rare. Drink bottled water or the alternative supply your water company provides. Boiling does not help.

This tool is for the times you have no safe supply and no instructions: for example stored water you are unsure of, or water from a source you would not normally drink.

## How the bleach dose is worked out

The tool multiplies your litres by the published dose per litre, then doubles it for cloudy water.

| Bleach strength | Clear water, per litre | Cloudy water, per litre |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5% to 9% | 0.1 ml (2 drops) | 0.2 ml (4 drops) |
| 1% | 0.5 ml (10 drops) | 1 ml (20 drops) |

Drops are millilitres divided by 0.05, which is the size of a typical drop. This matches the US CDC's own pairs (2 drops = 0.1 ml). Droppers vary, so trust the millilitres, especially for larger amounts.

After adding bleach, stir and wait at least 30 minutes. The water should smell slightly of chlorine. If it does not, the US EPA says to repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes. If it still does not smell of chlorine, use another method.

## What we left out, and why

- **Other bleach strengths.** The official doses cover 5% to 9% and 1% bleach only. For anything else, or a bottle you cannot read, the tool will not give a dose.
- **How many tablets per litre.** Tablets differ. The official advice is to follow the packet, so the tool gives you a timer you can set to the packet's waiting time.
- **A cooling time for boiled water.** The guidance says only to let it cool naturally.

## Where the figures come from

Every figure on this page was checked against these official pages on 25 October 2026. No UK source gives a household bleach dose, so the dose comes from US public health agencies.

- **Rolling boil, cool naturally, store in the fridge for up to 24 hours, what a notice covers, scalds:** Drinking Water Inspectorate, [Receiving a Boil Water Notice](https://www.dwi.gov.uk/receiving-a-boil-water-notice/).
- **1 minute at a rolling boil (3 minutes above 6,500 feet), bleach doses for 5% to 9% and 1%, double for cloudy water, wait 30 minutes, tablets, filters, contaminated water, storage:** US CDC, [How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html).
- **Bleach to avoid, the smell check, repeat and wait 15 minutes, bleach less than a year old:** US EPA, [Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water).
- **Never mix bleach with other cleaners:** US CDC, [Cleaning and Sanitizing with Bleach After an Emergency](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/44192).
