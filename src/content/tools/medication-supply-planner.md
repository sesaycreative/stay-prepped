## How the dates are worked out

For each medicine, the planner works out how many doses you have, then how many whole days they will last:

- **On hand** = full packs × the number in a pack, plus any loose ones.
- **Days left** = on hand ÷ how many you take a day, rounded down to whole days.
- **Runs out** = today plus the days left. That is the first day you will not have a full day's supply.
- **Order by** = the run-out date minus your lead time.

If the order-by date is today or has passed, the planner says **order now**. If you add your next repeat date and it falls after the run-out date, it warns you.

The planner counts down from the day you entered the amounts. If you come back a week later, it takes a week's doses off. When you count your packs again, enter the new numbers and the count starts from today.

## Why 7 days?

The NHS asks you to request a repeat prescription up to 5 working days before you need it. Five working days can be seven calendar days or more if a weekend or bank holiday falls in the middle, so 7 is the default. You can set a longer lead time if you want more margin, from 0 to 60 days.

## The colours

- **Green**, 14 days or more: you have time.
- **Amber**, 7 to 13 days: order soon if you have not already.
- **Red**, under 7 days: order now.

These bands are our planning convention, not an NHS rule. When at least one medicine is listed and none is red, the First aid area on your [progress page](/my-progress/) counts as ready.

## Medicines in an emergency

- Keep a written list of your medicines, the doses and your pharmacy's name in your [grab bag](/guides/72-hour-emergency-bag/) and with your [family emergency plan](/guides/family-emergency-plan/).
- If you completely run out, the NHS 111 online service can arrange a limited emergency supply. It cannot be used for antibiotics for a new problem or for controlled drugs that need ID.
- If anyone has a long-term health condition or relies on powered medical equipment, check whether they can join the free [Priority Services Register](https://www.thepsr.co.uk/).
